## Overview

A monitor re-checks pages on a schedule and compares each one with the previous check. Instead of polling a pricing page, a changelog or a policy page yourself, you get a webhook or an email with just the diff when something changes.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create">
    `POST /monitor` with what to watch, how often, and — optionally — a goal describing which changes matter.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Baseline">
    The first check records every page. Nothing is announced: every page is new by definition.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check">
    Each scheduled check fetches every page again and marks it `same`, `new`, `changed`, `removed` or `error`, with a diff.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Get told">
    Changes that match your goal are POSTed to your webhook and emailed to your addresses. You can also read any check with [`GET /monitor/{id}/checks/{checkId}`](/api-reference/endpoint/monitor-check).
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What to watch

A monitor has up to 50 **targets**, of three kinds. You can mix them.

| Target | Watches | Each check |
|---|---|---|
| `scrape` | A fixed list of up to 100 URLs | Fetches each page the way [`GET /scrape`](/api-reference/endpoint/scrape) would, rendering JavaScript and getting past bot protection |
| `crawl` | A site, or a section of one | Re-crawls it with the same options as [`POST /crawl`](/api-reference/endpoint/crawl), so pages that appear or disappear are reported too |
| `search` | Up to 5 web searches | Runs each search the way [`GET /search`](/api-reference/endpoint/search) would. A result the target has not returned before is `new`; one it has is `same` |

One check fetches at most **500 pages** across all targets — every scrape URL, every crawl's `limit`, and every search's result `limit`.

A search target watches the web rather than one site — news about a company, new posts on a topic:

```json
{
  "type": "search",
  "queries": ["acme corp funding round", "acme corp acquires"],
  "search_options": { "type": "news", "limit": 10, "time_range": "week" }
}
```

With a goal, each new result's page is read and judged, so only results that match it are announced (pass `scrape_results: false` to judge on the search snippet alone). Without a goal, every new result is announced. Results are recognised by URL, ignoring tracking parameters, and are never reported `removed`: a page dropping out of the results did not go anywhere.

<Note>
  A crawl can only report a page as `removed` when it runs out of pages to visit. A crawl that stops at its `limit` cannot tell a page that was taken down from one it never reached, so it reports no removals. Give a crawl target a `limit` comfortably above the section's size if removals matter to you.
</Note>

## Schedules

Write the schedule as a phrase or as five-field cron, in any IANA timezone:

```json
{ "schedule": { "text": "every weekday at 9am", "timezone": "America/New_York" } }
```

```json
{ "schedule": { "cron": "*/30 * * * *" } }
```

Phrases you can use include `every 30 minutes`, `every 15 minutes starting at :07`, `hourly`, `every 2 hours`, `daily`, `daily at 9am`, `daily at 5:30 PM`, `every weekday at 9am`, `weekly`, `every monday at 8:00` and `monthly`.

- Runs must be **as far apart as your plan requires** — every gap, not just the first: an hour on Free, 5 minutes on Starter and Pro, 2 on Growth, 1 on Scale.
- Phrases that leave the minute open (`hourly`, `daily`) get a fixed minute of their own, so monitors created at the same time do not all fire at once.
- The response always shows the cron the monitor actually runs on.
- Times follow the timezone across daylight-saving changes.

## Detecting changes

Each page of each check gets one status:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `same` | Fetched again; nothing changed. |
| `new` | Seen for the first time — on the first check, or a page that appeared since. |
| `changed` | Fetched again; the content differs. The page carries a `diff`. |
| `removed` | A page the monitor knew answered 404 or 410, or a crawl that ran to completion no longer finds it. Reported once. |
| `error` | Could not be checked. The last good snapshot is kept, so the next good check compares against it. |

By default the page's **markdown** is compared, and `diff.text` holds a unified diff with the same hunks structured in `diff.hunks`. Rendering noise never counts as a change: whitespace and line endings, invisible characters and non-breaking spaces, and the tracking, session and signature parameters in link URLs (`utm_source`, `fbclid`, `jsessionid`, signed CDN URLs). A page that loads but renders no text at all is reported as an `error`, not as every line removed.

To watch specific facts instead of the whole text, use the **json** mode. Describe the fields, and each check extracts them and compares them field by field:

```json
{
  "type": "scrape",
  "urls": ["https://example.com/pricing"],
  "change_tracking": { "modes": ["json"], "json_prompt": "Each plan: name, monthly price, headline features" }
}
```

A page is then `changed` only when a tracked field changes, and `diff.fields` says exactly which one: `plans[0].price: "$19/mo" → "$24/mo"`. Pass `json_schema` instead of, or as well as, `json_prompt` to fix the shape. Use `"modes": ["json", "markdown"]` to get both diffs.

The extraction is read again only when the page's text changed: an unchanged page keeps its last extraction, so a model reading identical text a little differently is never a change (and costs nothing). List items are matched by identity — an `id`, `url`, `name` or `title` field — so one job inserted at the top of a list is one `added` field, not every job shifted.

### Cutting noise

Most false alarms come from page furniture: a "last updated" date, a rotating testimonial, a visitor counter. These `change_tracking` options keep them quiet:

| Option | What it does |
|---|---|
| `ignore_text` | Up to 25 phrases; lines containing one are left out of the comparison. Plain text, case-insensitive, `*` for anything: `["people are viewing", "viewed * times"]`. |
| `ignore_dates` | Dates, times and "3 minutes ago" are masked out before comparing. |
| `ignore_order` | Lines that only moved are not a change. |
| `notify_on` | Which kinds of change notify: `added`, `removed`, `changed` lines or fields (for a crawl, pages that appear or disappear; a tracked price or number that moves is `changed`). `["added"]` tells you about new job posts, not filled ones. |
| `only_new_content` | Notify only when a change brings text the page has not shown in its recent history, so content that cycles (a carousel, A/B variants) stays quiet. |

`ignore_text`, `ignore_dates` and `ignore_order` change only what is compared: a change still shows the page as it reads. `notify_on` and `only_new_content` decide whether a change found notifies; the page is still recorded as `changed`, with `diff.alert` saying why it was held back. Changing one of these options re-baselines the target's pages silently instead of reporting them all changed.

<Tip>
  You can also leave parts of the page out with `scrape_options.exclude_selectors`, or switch to the json mode and track only what you care about. Images are left out of the comparison by default, because their URLs change with every deploy.
</Tip>

## Goals and judging

Add a `goal` — one plain sentence about what you want to be told — and every changed, new or removed page is judged against it:

```json
{ "goal": "Alert me when a plan price or its included features change" }
```

Each judged page gets `is_meaningful` and a `judgment` with a `reason`. Pages ruled **not meaningful** are still recorded on the check, but don't send a webhook or an email. Good goals restate their scope ("the Pro plan", "the top 10", "API changes") and name exclusions only when they matter.

Judging is on whenever a goal is set; pass `judge_enabled: false` to keep a goal without judging. The first check's baseline is never judged.

- The goal decides what matters: if it asks for something that is usually noise (a timestamp, a counter), it gets it; if it says to ignore something, that thing never counts.
- The page's content is treated as data, never as instructions — text on a page cannot talk the judge out of an alert.
- On a long diff the judge reads the hunks most relevant to your goal, not just the first ones.
- `meaningful_changes` quote the page exactly; a quote the judge was not shown is dropped.
- A page that flips back to a version it has shown before is not judged again: the earlier verdict is reused, free.

## Webhooks

With a `webhook`, two events are POSTed to you:

| Event | When |
|---|---|
| [`monitor.page`](/api-reference/endpoint/monitor-webhook-page) | A page changed, appeared, was removed, or started failing — and, with a goal, the judge ruled it meaningful |
| [`monitor.check.completed`](/api-reference/endpoint/monitor-webhook-check) | A check finished, with its summary counts |

Every request is signed with the monitor's `webhook.secret`. Answer `2xx` within 10 seconds; failures are retried after 1, 5 and 15 minutes. The webhook URL must use `https://`.

A page that fails sends one `monitor.page` event, when it starts failing. Set `notify_errors_after` (1–10) to send it only once a page has failed that many checks in a row, so a site that blips once and recovers sends nothing.

```json
{
  "webhook": {
    "url": "https://you.example.org/hooks/prefetch",
    "headers": { "Authorization": "Bearer your-token" },
    "metadata": { "env": "production" },
    "events": ["monitor.page"]
  }
}
```

## Email alerts

Add `email` to have changes emailed to up to 5 addresses:

```json
{ "email": { "to": ["you@example.com", "teammate@example.com"] } }
```

Each check that finds changes sends **one email** to every active address, listing the pages that changed, appeared or were removed, with the judge's reason and the changed lines or fields. The same rules as the `monitor.page` webhook decide what counts: the baseline is never emailed, and with a goal, changes ruled not meaningful are left out. A page that fails to load is counted in the email, not emailed on its own. Up to 20 pages are listed; the rest are counted.

Before anything is sent, each address has to agree to it:

| Address | What happens |
|---|---|
| The email of your Prefetch account, or of anyone in your organisation | `active` at once. |
| Any other address | Sent one confirmation email and `pending` until its owner clicks **Confirm**. The link expires after 7 days. |

Every email has an unsubscribe link, and supports one-click unsubscribe in mail clients that offer it. An address that unsubscribes is shown as `unsubscribed` and gets nothing more from that monitor, even if you keep it in the list. To ask it again, remove the address and add it back.

`email.recipients` in the response shows each address's status. If a pending address never got its confirmation, send it again with a `PATCH`:

```json
{ "email": { "to": ["teammate@example.com"], "resend_confirmation": true } }
```

<Note>
  An address can be asked to confirm at most 3 times a day, from any number of monitors, and an API key can send at most 20 confirmation emails a day. A request that would go over either limit is refused with `429` and changes nothing.
</Note>

Emails are free: they never cost credits.

## Credits

Creating, reading and changing a monitor is free. **Each check** is billed to the API key that created the monitor:

- **1 credit per page fetched** — the [`/scrape`](/api-reference/endpoint/scrape) rate, plus 3 when a `json` target's extraction runs (only when the page's text changed).
- **2 credits per search** a `search` target runs — the [`/search`](/api-reference/endpoint/search) rate — plus 1 per new result read.
- **1 credit per page judged** against your goal — changed, new or removed pages only. A verdict reused for a change judged before is free.

Pages that fail to load are never charged, and neither are skipped checks. `estimated_credits_per_month` is an upper bound: it assumes every page is fetched and judged on every check.

A check never spends more than the key has left. When credits run out partway, it stops and reports `partial`; when the key has none at all, the check is recorded as `skipped_no_credits` and the monitor keeps its schedule, resuming as soon as credits refill.

<Warning>
  A monitor bills on every check for as long as it is active. A crawl target with `limit: 100` on a 5-minute schedule fetches 28,800 pages a day. Start small, and pause monitors you are not using.
</Warning>

A monitor whose every page has failed for 10 checks in a row, over at least 24 hours, is paused, and its email recipients are told. Failed pages are never billed, so this only stops retrying a page that is gone or blocks every fetch. Set `status` back to `"active"` once the pages load.

## Limits

| Limit | Value |
|---|---|
| Targets per monitor | 50 |
| URLs per scrape target | 100 |
| Searches per search target | 5, of up to 20 results each |

The rest depend on your plan:

| Limit | Free | Starter | Pro | Growth | Scale |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shortest schedule | Hourly | Every 5 minutes | Every 5 minutes | Every 2 minutes | Every minute |
| Monitors per API key | 5 | 50 | 250 | 1,000 | 5,000 |
| Pages per check | 10 | 500 | 500 | 500 | 500 |
| Check history (`retention_days`, 30 by default) | Up to 30 days | Up to 365 | Up to 365 | Up to 365 | Up to 365 |
| Email addresses per monitor | 1, on your account | 5 | 5 | 5 | 5 |
| Pages that block bots | Not retried | Retried through a stealth browser and proxies | Retried | Retried | Retried |

A request that asks for more than your plan allows is refused, and the error names the limit. A monitor made on a bigger plan keeps running after a downgrade: a schedule that is now too frequent moves to the fastest your plan allows (its email recipients are told once), and its history is cut to your plan's window.

<Note>
  Monitors need a direct Prefetch API key. They are not available through RapidAPI, which meters usage from a response header — and a scheduled check runs when there is no response to carry one.
</Note>
